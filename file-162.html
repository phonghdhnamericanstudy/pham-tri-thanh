01 / Thanh’s Journey

Thanh’s
journey.

Born in 2009, I grew up with LEGO bricks, people who encouraged my curiosity, and the joy of making something work.

01Early curiosity

From LEGO bricks to prototypes.

My love for STEM began with childhood LEGO bricks and a supportive mom who always created opportunities for me to explore my curiosity. When she enrolled me in my first technology and coding classes, I instantly felt at home. Loving communication and teamwork, I quickly bonded with new friends, and almost immediately, we were building our very first tech prototypes together. The thrill of bringing those early creations to life sparked an endless drive in me to keep inventing and crafting new things.

02Discovering physics

Understanding how things work.

Science has fascinated me since elementary school. Physics, in particular, offers a way to understand the logic behind everyday things, beyond the numbers and equations on a page.

As I grew older, my goal shifted from understanding the world to finding ways to make it better. I want to turn theory into tangible machines that can support families in remote highlands and help young students on their journey to school.

03People & purpose

Why I want to build.

My journey began with a sense of responsibility toward my own community, and every product I build stems from the smallest stirrings of empathy. At heart, I simply want to help people to bring electricity to families in remote highlands, and to give young children libraries filled with enough books to study and explore every day, just as I had growing up. Above all, I dream of a world where people are deeply connected, where everyone lives in warmth and happiness, and has the freedom to pursue what they truly love.

That motivation shapes the projects I choose: monitoring local heat, responding to rising water, storing energy, and exploring ways to improve solar power.

04What I learned at home

A family connection to service.

My parents’ work with local NGOs in mountainous border communities introduced me to projects involving clean water and support for women’s livelihoods. Their commitment helped shape my own sense of responsibility to the community.

My grandfather’s resilience is another source of inspiration. After losing his right arm during the war, he learned to write with his left and went on to work with agricultural tools and machinery. As a child, I spent time with him in the countryside and helped repair household items. Those moments connected my interest in making things with the practical value they can bring to someone’s life.
