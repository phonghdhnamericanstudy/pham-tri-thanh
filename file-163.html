01 / Owner / Developer

PaveGuard

On a hot day in Vietnam, the conditions on a particular street can feel very different from a regional weather forecast. From inside an air-conditioned home, it can be difficult to know how hot the road outside really is.

We developed PaveGuard to measure road surface temperature and humidity, helping residents check local street conditions and take precautions before heading out.

02 / Co-owner / STEMese

FloodGuard

News of severe storms and flash floods made our team think about how a practical device might help communities respond sooner. We focused on one of the clearest indicators of flood risk: rising water levels.

Our team engineered a monitoring device designed to alert residents when water rises, giving people more time to respond. The prototype uses solar power and was designed with rain, humidity, and outdoor conditions in mind.

03 / Developer / Energy storage

Sand Battery

Our team noticed a contrast: heat and sunlight can go unused, while many households in remote highlands still need reliable sources of warmth. We came together to explore a sand battery that could store thermal energy.

The prototype contains a chamber filled with sand and aluminum pipes. Heat transferred through the pipes warms the surrounding sand, while insulation made with cotton wool and foam helps retain that energy.

We explored how stored heat might support everyday needs such as warming water, heating living spaces, and assisting with cooking. Building the prototype also taught us how carefully every structural detail and scientific principle must be explained.

04 / Research / Solar energy

Solar Tracker

During a community trip to Con Cuông, Nghệ An, I saw families facing intense heat while rugged terrain made grid electricity difficult to access. After installing solar panels and observing their output through a web dashboard, I began thinking about how to capture sunlight more effectively.

Watching children spin wooden tops gave me a new way to think about movement and balance. That observation became part of my motivation to explore a solar-tracking system and the possibility of improving panel output while keeping a design practical for local households.

05 / Engineering Intern

Hanoi University of Science and Technology

At HUST’s School of Electrical and Electronic Engineering, I worked on projects and assignments that introduced me to coding, product development, hardware assembly, robotics, and IoT.

Learning alongside university students and professors showed me how different the university environment could be. The fast pace and expectation of independence felt challenging at first, but I gradually learned to keep up and take more responsibility for my work.

06 / Engineering Intern

ASO Mechatronics Company

I spent a term learning and working at ASO’s factory workshop in Thái Nguyên, where engineers and technicians manufacture custom machinery for other companies and factories.

Working alongside experienced craftspeople gave me practical exposure to electronics, automation, transformers, and central control units. I learned more about assembly, soldering, and troubleshooting electrical hazards, and gained a clearer sense of the care that hands-on engineering requires.
